Independent Power Producer (IPP) Opportunities

Power Purchase by Sabah Electricity Sdn. Bhd.
Complete Guide 2025
Document Overview: This comprehensive guide provides detailed information about IPP opportunities, licensing requirements, technical specifications, and power purchase agreements with Sabah Electricity Sdn. Bhd. (SESB) in Sabah, Malaysia.

1Current IPP Opportunities in Sabah

1.1 Large-Scale Solar (LSS) Program

Energy Commission of Sabah (ECoS) has approved 15 large-scale solar projects with a total capacity of 199 MW under LSS Sabah 2024. These projects are distributed across the West Coast and East Coast of Sabah.

1.2 Industrial Area IPP Policy

The state government now allows management of new industrial areas to build their own power plants or Independent Power Producers (IPP). This approach enables industrial areas to generate their own energy without relying entirely on SESB.

1.3 Major Renewable Energy Projects

Hydroelectric Projects:

  • Ulu Padas Hydroelectric Project
  • Hydroelectric potential exceeding 1 GW has been identified
  • Target: 2,000 MW by 2037 (38% of grid capacity mix)
  • Project Oriole: 162 MW run-of-river hydro (commissioning 2028)

Solar Energy:

  • Sabah has an estimated solar capacity of 4 GW with the highest solar irradiation values in Malaysia
  • Projects in Kudat, Beaufort, Tawau, and Sandakan

Biomass:

  • 129 palm oil mills produce 26.2 million tons of biomass annually
  • Only 5% currently utilized - significant development opportunity

Geothermal:

  • Geothermal development being revived in Tawau area
  • Potential: 100 MW

1.4 Medium-Term Projects

A 160MW LNG-based power plant in Tawau has been approved by the Sabah Energy Council, targeted for completion by 2028.

1.5 Recent Power Purchase Agreements

Coastal Contracts Bhd signed a 25-year Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with SESB for a 15MW solar project in Sandakan in June 2025.

2IPP Purchase Status

Key Statistic: 50.3% of total energy units generated are purchased from Independent Power Producers (IPP), indicating SESB's significant reliance on IPPs for electricity supply.

3.3 Processing Timeline

  • Applications should be submitted at least 3 months before Initial Operation Date (IOD)
  • Subject to complete documentation, applications will be approved within 15 working days

4Documentation Requirements

4.1 Company Documents

Applicants must submit statutory documents under the Companies Act 1966 (certified true copies):

  • Form 9 - Certificate of Incorporation
  • Form 13 - Certificate of Incorporation on Change of Name (if any)
  • Form 24 - Return of Allotment of Shares
  • Form 32A - Return for Transfer of Shares Transaction (if any)
  • Form 44 - Notice of Situation of Registered Office
  • Form 49 - Return of Directors, Managers and Secretaries
  • Form 55 - Form of Annual Return
  • Memorandum and Articles of Association

4.2 Technical Documents

  • Location plan and site plan (A4 or A3 size)
  • High voltage and low voltage schematic drawings
  • Single Line Diagram endorsed by Professional Engineer

4.3 Financial Documents

  • Letter of intent from financial institution for project financing
  • For self-funded projects: Recent three-month financial statements
  • Project financial template (provided by ECoS)

4.4 Approval Documents

  • Approval letters from government agencies
  • Local authority approvals
  • SESB approval letter
  • Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) if required

4.5 Post-Commissioning Documents

  • Form F - Certificate of Supervision and Completion
  • Form G - Testing Certificate
  • Copy of registered Competent Person certificate

5Technical Requirements

5.1 Standards and Quality

All designs, plans, specifications and details must be approved by qualified engineers registered as Professional Engineers with the Board of Engineers, Malaysia.

5.2 Competent Person Requirements

  • Plants must be operated by registered Competent Persons
  • Forms F and G must be provided by appointed competent person

5.3 Quality Inspection

  • ECoS may conduct quality assurance inspections and performance tests
  • All inspection costs are borne by the applicant

5.4 Voltage and Frequency Standards

The voltage, frequency and quality of electricity supplied must be kept within ranges specified in the Regulations and Grid Code or as determined by the Commission.

8Government Policies and Priorities

8.1 Technology Priorities

The Government has outlined important policies related to the electricity supply industry:

  • Diversification of fuel consumption
  • Reduce dependence on particular fuel with additional use of hydro and solar
  • Use of renewable energy (biomass, industrial waste, solid waste)
  • Use of advanced or efficient technology such as cogeneration
  • Use of less polluting electricity generation technologies
  • Promote development of new technologies or methods

8.2 Bumiputera Participation

Licensees must take cognizance of Government policies particularly:

  • Bumiputera equity participation
  • Transfer of technology and expertise to Malaysians
  • Employment of local personnel, contractors and services
  • Use of local materials and products

9Regulatory Framework

9.1 Energy Commission of Sabah (ECoS)

Sabah gained full autonomy over the electricity sector in January 2024 through the establishment of ECoS, enabling direct oversight of licensing, approvals, and policy implementation.

9.2 Compliance Requirements

  • Performance Monitoring: Monthly reports on maximum daily load, electricity sold, plant efficiency, fuel usage, breakdowns, and maintenance schedules
  • Environmental Compliance: Must comply with all EIA requirements and Director General of Environmental Quality directives
  • Grid Code: Must comply with Single Buyer Rules and Sabah Grid Code
  • Insurance: Must insure the plant throughout the license period

9.3 Penalties

Legal Consequences:
  • Failure to comply with Section 8(1)(a): Fine up to RM 500,000 or imprisonment for 5 years
  • Operating without license Section 8(1)(b): Fine up to RM 1 million or imprisonment for 10 years
  • Non-compliance with codes/guidelines: Fine up to RM 200,000 or imprisonment for 2 years

10Key Points to Remember

  1. Submit applications at least 3 months before IOD
  2. All designs must be approved by Professional Engineers
  3. Competent Person must operate the facility
  4. Monthly performance reports are mandatory
  5. Environmental compliance is strictly enforced
  6. Grid Code compliance is required at all times
  7. Insurance coverage is mandatory throughout license period
  8. License is non-transferable without Minister's approval
